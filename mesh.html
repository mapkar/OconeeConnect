How the mesh works

LoRa radios · planned MeshCore backbone

Think of it as passing a short note across a room of radios. Your handheld does not need to reach Walhalla by itself. It needs to reach the next node that can.

LoRa

  • LoRa means Long Range: a slow, narrow radio mode that can travel miles on very little power.
  • In the US, including South Carolina, this is the licence-exempt 915 MHz ISM band (902–928 MHz). FCC Part 15. No ham ticket required at legal power.
  • Devices are small, cheap, and run for days on a battery — or continuously with solar.

What you actually do

  1. You carry or mount a LoRa node.
  2. Your phone talks to that node over Bluetooth (sometimes USB or Wi-Fi).
  3. You type a short message in the app.
  4. The radio sends it. Nearby infrastructure hears it and forwards it.
  5. The note hops until it reaches the person or room you meant.

MeshCore vs Meshtastic

Same class of boards. Two different firmwares. They do not exchange messages over the air.

OconeeConnect is building infrastructure: solar repeaters on purpose-chosen high spots. That is why the backbone is MeshCore. If you only want a weekend trail radio with friends, Meshtastic is still a good tool — just know it is a separate network. Full Meshtastic walkthrough: Meshtastic guide.

Radio plan for this mesh

Initial rollout uses North American MeshCore settings with a higher spreading factor (SF11) so thin coverage still links. As more repeaters come up, spreading factor comes down (target toward SF5) so messages spend less time on the air.

Every node that wants to hear the OconeeConnect MeshCore backbone must use the same frequency, bandwidth, and spreading factor as that backbone. Wrong preset = silence.
