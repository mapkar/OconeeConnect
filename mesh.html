How the mesh works

LoRa radios · MeshCore backbone

Think of it as passing a short note from radio to radio. Your handheld doesn’t need to reach Walhalla by itself — it just needs to reach the next repeater that can help carry the message.

LoRa

  • LoRa stands for Long Range: a slow, narrow radio mode that can travel miles on very little power.
  • In the US (including South Carolina) this is the license-exempt 915 MHz ISM band (902–928 MHz). No ham license required at legal power levels.
  • The devices are small, inexpensive, and can run for days on a battery — or continuously with a little solar.

What you actually do

  1. You carry or mount a LoRa node running MeshCore Companion firmware.
  2. Your phone talks to that node over Bluetooth (sometimes USB or Wi-Fi).
  3. You type a short message in the MeshCore app.
  4. The radio sends it on the USA/Canada preset. Nearby repeaters on the same settings hear it and may forward it.
  5. The note hops until it reaches the person you meant, or it runs out of path.

Why MeshCore

OconeeConnect uses one firmware: MeshCore. It is purpose-built for efficient mesh messaging with dedicated repeaters carrying the load while companions stay quiet.

Radio plan

We use the USA/Canada (Recommended) preset. Frequency 910.525 MHz, bandwidth 62.5 kHz, spreading factor 7, coding rate 5. This is the standard for North American MeshCore networks and keeps airtime reasonable while providing good range.

Every node that wants to hear OconeeConnect needs MeshCore firmware and the USA/Canada preset. Wrong firmware or wrong radio settings means silence.
