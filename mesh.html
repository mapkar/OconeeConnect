How the mesh works

LoRa radios · Meshtastic backbone

Think of it as passing a short note across a room of radios. Your handheld does not need to reach Walhalla by itself. It needs to reach the next node that can.

LoRa

  • LoRa means Long Range: a slow, narrow radio mode that can travel miles on very little power.
  • In the US, including South Carolina, this is the licence-exempt 915 MHz ISM band (902–928 MHz). FCC Part 15. No ham ticket required at legal power.
  • Devices are small, cheap, and run for days on a battery — or continuously with solar.

What you actually do

  1. You carry or mount a LoRa node running Meshtastic.
  2. Your phone talks to that node over Bluetooth (sometimes USB or Wi-Fi).
  3. You type a short message in the Meshtastic app.
  4. The radio sends it. Nearby nodes on the same region, preset, and channel hear it and may forward it.
  5. The note hops until it reaches the person you meant, or it runs out of hops.

Why Meshtastic

OconeeConnect is one firmware. Meshtastic is the one locals already run, the one South Carolina Mesh publishes, and the one a handheld can join without a private repeater protocol.

Radio plan

Start on LongFast. It is the Meshtastic default and the usual public landing (frequency slot 20 if you set it explicitly). Hop limit stays at 3 until the Seneca / Walhalla / Poplar Springs routers are mapped. Faster presets are a later congestion tool, not the join setting.

Every node that wants to hear OconeeConnect must use Meshtastic, region US, and the same primary channel. Wrong firmware or wrong region = silence.
