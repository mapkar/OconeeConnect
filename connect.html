How to connect

Join the OconeeConnect Meshtastic mesh

Most people are on the air in 15–30 minutes once they have a real data USB cable. Detailed channel and role notes are on the Settings page.

  1. Get a 915 MHz board. Heltec V3/V4, RAK4631 / RAK19003, T-Beam, or Seeed trackers all work. Just confirm the listing says US / 915 MHz.
  2. Attach the antenna first. Then power the board. (Always antenna before power.)
  3. Flash Meshtastic. On a computer, open Chrome or Edge (Safari and Firefox usually won’t work for flashing). Go to flasher.meshtastic.org. Plug in via USB, pick your board, and flash the latest stable build. If the board previously ran MeshCore or an older Meshtastic version, choose the full erase option on the first flash.
  4. Install the Meshtastic app. iOS App Store or Android Play Store. You can also use the browser client at client.meshtastic.org.
  5. Set the radio. Pair over Bluetooth. Set LoRa region to US, preset to LongFast, and frequency slot to 20 (instead of leaving it at 0, so renaming the channel later doesn’t move you off the public frequency). Hop limit 3. Role CLIENT unless this is an agreed solar site. If you don’t hear anyone right away, double-check region, slot, and firmware — or ask on X @0k_sashes before changing other settings.
  6. Name the node. Something that says who and roughly where you are (town helps). Send a short test on the primary channel once another node is in range.

If nothing appears

  • Region, frequency slot, or firmware doesn’t match the rest of the mesh. This is the most common reason a radio seems silent.
  • Charge-only USB cable. The flasher never sees the board.
  • You’re in a low spot and no router hears you yet. That’s a coverage question, not a broken radio. See the Coverage page.
  • The board is still running MeshCore. Just reflash Meshtastic — the two firmwares don’t exchange messages.
A private household channel (with a QR code) will be published once the first two hubs are stable on the air. Until then, LongFast on slot 20 is the public place to meet. A random secondary key is not “the Oconee channel.”
