Help it grow

Sites matter more than slogans

OconeeConnect is locals helping locals. The scarce resource is not firmware. It is height with a view and a landlord who will let a small solar box stay there.

1. Host a node

  • Rooftop, shop, church, school pole, farm ridge, lake house.
  • Best: line of sight toward Seneca, Walhalla, or the US 28 corridor.
  • Solar preferred so the site does not need a spare outlet.

We can talk through mount style, wind, and access before anything goes up. Site owners should expect a small, removable box — not a cell tower.

2. Run your own companion

Buy a handheld, flash MeshCore, and live on the mesh. Every person who can hear a hub makes the network useful the day the hub comes online. How to connect.

3. Gear that actually helps

  • US 915 MHz nodes (Heltec, RAK, LilyGO, Seeed solar).
  • Tuned 915 MHz antennas.
  • Small panels, LiPo packs, weather housings, pole hardware.

4. Tell the right people

Hikers, boat clubs, ham clubs, neighborhood chats, and anyone who lost signal in Helene. Public contact: @0k_sashes on X.

Outreach to billboard owners, SCDOT right-of-way, and school sites is underway as separate conversations. Those stay off this page until a site is actually approved.
